3D on 4D viewer

P unit balls (radius 1) or unit cubes (edge 1) on the boundary of a tesseract, the 4D cube. Its boundary is eight solid cubes, its cells, glued face to face. A piece may cross a square face into the next cell, but no edge of the tesseract may pass through it. The score is the tesseract's edge s; smaller is better.

How to read the drawing
  • Cells shows the eight cubes of the boundary side by side, each in its own coordinates. A face's colour names the cell glued to it there.
  • Net lays the cells out in one piece, a cross of cubes. Fold folds it up into the fourth dimension, seen from a 4D eye: fully folded, it is the classic picture of the tesseract, a small cube inside a large one.
  • Slice cuts the tesseract with a hyperplane across one axis. Inside, the slice of the boundary is the surface of a cube, and each piece shows its cross-section on it; at either end the slice is a whole cell. Move the position to sweep through, or show a strip of nine slices.
  • Drag to turn the drawing, or focus it and use the arrow keys (Shift for larger steps), + and − to zoom, Page Up and Page Down to follow one piece, Home to reset. Point at or tap a piece to see where it lies.
  • The address names the problem shown, so a link reopens it.
