What the pictures show

Each problem puts P unit pieces (disks of diameter 1, or regular polygons of side 1) into the smallest container on a surface of constant curvature K: the hyperbolic plane for K < 0, the flat plane at K = 0, a sphere of radius 1/√K for K > 0. The size s is the container's side (its radius for a disk container). The plots show the smallest s found at each K, and every configuration that was followed continuously in K.

  • Branch: one arrangement of the pieces, followed as K changes. Each step re-optimises the previous arrangement at the new K, so the pieces move continuously.
  • Thick curve: the lowest branch at each K, which is the ground state found. Its colour is the branch that holds the lead.
  • Dashed curve: a branch that is not the lowest there. It is metastable: a local optimum that a slow change of K can still follow. Where a branch stays alive past the point where it lost the lead, the system shows hysteresis: which arrangement you see depends on the direction you came from.

Events

  • ◆ Crossing: two branches exchange the lead. The best size s*(K) stays continuous, but its slope jumps (a kink), and the optimal arrangement jumps from one configuration to a different one. This is a first-order transition, like a level crossing.
  • ○ Contact change: along one branch a contact opens or closes, or a piece becomes loose (a rattler). The slope is usually continuous and only the curvature of s(K) changes; it kinks only when the contact forces are not unique.
  • Stability change: a symmetric branch stops being a local optimum. A small push lowers s, so the optimum breaks the symmetry. This is the packing version of a pitchfork bifurcation.
  • × Fold: the followed branch stops existing. Re-optimising then jumps to another arrangement with a smaller s.
  • | End: at negative K the container would have to grow beyond the ideal polygon (s → ∞). At positive K it would leave the open hemisphere.

How it works

Conformal picture. A point at geodesic distance r from the container centre, in direction φ, is drawn at radius 2 tnK(r/2), where tnK(x) = tan(√K x)/√K for K > 0, tanh(√−K x)/√−K for K < 0, and x at K = 0. This is the Poincaré disk for K < 0 and stereographic projection for K > 0, scaled so that it is exact at the centre. Angles are true, circles stay circles, and the picture changes continuously through K = 0. In homogeneous coordinates (v0, w) with v0² + K|w|² = 1, the drawn point is 2w/(1 + v0). The dashed circle is the boundary at infinity (K < 0) or the equator (K > 0). The grey outline is the ideal polygon, which the container approaches as s → ∞. The "Klein / gnomonic" button shows the other chart, where edges are straight but shapes are distorted.

Search. For each problem, seeded multistart searches run every 0.25 in K. They are seeded with the Euclidean record at K = 0 and with the curved records at K = ±1. Every competitive configuration is followed in both directions on a grid of step 1/64, with step halving near trouble. A step is rejected when the configuration jumps and s drops, and below a step of 2−26 the branch ends in a fold. Refinement rounds then perturb the lowest configuration at every second grid value and follow anything lower. Crossings, contact changes and stability changes are located by bisection in K on polished configurations. Slopes come from one-sided finite differences.

Proof of validity. The ground state every 1/16 in K, and both branches at every crossing (at Kc and at the grid values on either side), are re-solved with a margin and checked by the independent certifier python/curved/certify.py (256-bit interval arithmetic). Each such size is therefore a proven upper bound for its K. Nothing is claimed optimal. A jump in the best-found curve counts only when both branches were followed through the crossing from both sides.
